## Key takeaways

There are two ways to connect an SD card to a computer. If the computer has an SD slot, insert the card directly. If it doesn't, put the card in an SD card reader for PC and plug the reader into a USB port.

Even with a slot, a slot built to an older standard than your card may not read it. In that case, use a USB reader that supports the card's standard.

Once connected, copy files in Windows File Explorer or the Mac Finder. On Windows, you can also move photos with Import in the Photos app. When you're done, eject the card before you pull it out.

"How do I connect my camera's SD card to my computer?" "My laptop doesn't have an SD slot." This guide covers the basic steps to connect a card and move files. For how to pick a reader, see [how to choose an SD card reader](/blog/en/choose-sd-card-reader).

## Do you need an SD card reader for PC?

The [SD Association FAQ](https://www.sdcard.org/consumers/faq/) says that if your computer has an SD slot, you can transfer data by inserting the card. If it has no slot, or the slot is built to an earlier SD standard than your card, it recommends a USB reader/writer or a card adapter.

| Method | When to use it | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The computer's SD slot | Your laptop or desktop has an SD slot | Whether the slot supports the card's capacity standard |
| USB SD card reader | There's no slot, or the slot's standard is too old | The reader's plug (USB-A or USB-C) and supported standards |
| microSD adapter | You want to put a microSD card in an SD slot | The card's standard stays the same with an adapter |

For microSD and adapters, see [micro SD card vs SD card](/blog/en/microsd-vs-sd). If your laptop only has USB-C ports, also see [how to use a USB-C SD card reader](/blog/en/usb-c-sd-card-reader).

## Can your computer read this card?

Match the card's capacity standard to the slot or reader. The [SD Association's capacity guide](https://www.sdcard.org/consumers/about-sd-memory-card-choices/sd-sdhc-sdxc-and-sduc-card-capacity-choices/) lists compatibility like this.

| Card | Slots and readers that can use it |
| --- | --- |
| SD | SD, SDHC, SDXC, SDUC |
| SDHC | SDHC, SDXC, SDUC |
| SDXC | SDXC, SDUC |
| SDUC | SDUC |

The same guide says to check the device's manual or manufacturer information for supported standards before inserting a card. For a laptop's SD slot, look in the computer maker's spec sheet. Capacity ranges for each standard are in [SDHC vs SDXC](/blog/en/sdhc-vs-sdxc).

Apple publishes a model list for Mac. According to [Apple's guide to the Mac SD card slot](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102352), these models have an SDXC slot.

| Mac type | Models with an SDXC slot |
| --- | --- |
| Mac Studio | 2022 or later |
| 14-inch and 16-inch MacBook Pro | 2021 or later |
| MacBook Pro | 2011 through 2015 |
| Mac mini | 2011 through 2014 |
| 13-inch MacBook Air | 2013 through 2017 |
| 27-inch and 21.5-inch iMac | 2010 or later |

For a Mac not on this list, use a USB reader.

## How to open an SD card on a Windows PC

1. Check the card's write-protect switch. To delete or save files, set it to the unlocked position.
2. Insert the card into the PC's SD slot, or put it in a reader and plug the reader into a USB port. Follow your PC's manual for which way the card goes into the slot.
3. Open File Explorer and find the card's drive.
4. Copy the files from the card to a folder on your PC.

To check the switch position, see [the SD card lock switch](/blog/en/sd-card-lock-switch).

What pops up when you insert a card depends on your AutoPlay settings. According to [Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/how-to-install-programs-from-a-disc), you turn AutoPlay on or off at Start > Settings > Bluetooth & devices > AutoPlay. You can also choose the default action for removable drives and memory cards on the same page.

## How to import photos from an SD card with the Windows Photos app

[Microsoft's Photos app guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/photos/manage-photos-and-videos-with-microsoft-photos-app) says you can import photos and videos from a camera's SD card, a USB drive, or a phone. The import steps follow [Microsoft's import guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/import-photos-and-videos-aed09800-f826-4d40-a243-7640de229d9d).

1. Select the Start button and open the Photos app.
2. Select Import, then choose the card from your connected devices.
3. Pick the items you want to import and where to save them.
4. Follow the on-screen instructions to finish.

To keep file names and folder structure as they are, copying in File Explorer is simpler. Use the Photos app when you want to pick which items to bring over.

## How to connect an SD card to a Mac

These steps follow [Apple's guide to the Mac SD card slot](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102352).

1. Insert the card with the metal contacts facing down and pointing toward the computer. Don't force it in.
2. When the card's icon appears, open the files in the Finder.
3. When you're done, drag the card's icon to the Trash to eject it.
4. After the icon disappears, remove the card.

The same guide lists these slot conditions.

| Item | Apple's guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Supported cards | MMC, Default Speed, High Speed, UHS-I |
| UHS-II support | Mac Studio 2022 or later, MacBook Pro 2021 or later, iMac 2020, iMac Pro |
| miniSD, microSD | Use an adapter |
| Not supported | SDIO cards |
| Caution | Cards thicker than 2.1 mm might damage the slot |

## How do you remove an SD card safely?

On Windows, follow [Microsoft's guide to safely removing hardware](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/safely-remove-hardware-in-windows-1ee6677d-4e6c-4359-efca-fd44b9cec369). Microsoft says to always safely remove devices before disconnecting them to avoid losing data.

1. In the system tray area of the taskbar, right-click the Safely Remove Hardware and Eject Media icon. If you don't see it, select Show hidden icons first.
2. In the menu, select Eject for the card's drive.
3. When the Safe To Remove Hardware notification appears, remove the card or reader.

On a Mac, drag the card's icon to the Trash. Apple warns that removing a card while the Mac is asleep could lead to data loss. Wake the computer and eject the card before you remove it.

## What if your computer doesn't show the SD card?

| What you see | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| The card's icon doesn't appear on a Mac | Remove the card and insert it again. Apple says a card may not mount if you insert it too slowly or too quickly |
| A Mac says "cannot be modified" | Eject the card, unlock the lock switch, and reinsert it |
| The slot doesn't show the card, but a reader does | Check whether the slot supports the card's standard |
| The Safely Remove Hardware icon is missing on Windows | Microsoft says the icon may not appear if no device is connected or Windows doesn't recognize it |

If a window asks you to format the card or errors keep coming back, don't format right away. Work through [what to check when an SD card isn't working](/blog/en/sd-card-not-working) first. If you do need to format, see [how to use the SD Memory Card Formatter](/blog/en/sd-card-formatter).

## SD card reader for PC FAQ

### What if my laptop has no SD slot?

Use a USB reader. The SD Association recommends a USB reader/writer or a card adapter for computers without a slot. Pick a reader that fits your laptop's port (USB-A or USB-C).

### Can I put a microSD card straight into my computer's slot?

An SD slot needs an adapter that turns the microSD card into SD size. Apple also says to use an adapter for microSD cards in a Mac slot.

### Can I just pull the card out without ejecting?

That's not recommended. Microsoft says to safely remove devices before disconnecting them to avoid losing data. Apple also says to eject the card before removing it.

### If my slot only supports UHS-I, can I still use a UHS-II card?

Yes. The [SD Association's bus interface guide](https://www.sdcard.org/consumers/about-sd-memory-card-choices/bus-interface-speed-standards-for-large-size-data-transfer/) says faster cards can be used in UHS-I devices. You just won't get UHS-II speed. For the differences, see [UHS-I vs UHS-II vs SD Express](/blog/en/uhs-sd-card).

### My old computer can't read a 64 GB card.

A 64 GB card is SDXC. According to the SD Association, SDXC cards can only be used with SDXC or SDUC devices. If your slot only supports SDHC, use a USB reader that supports SDXC.
