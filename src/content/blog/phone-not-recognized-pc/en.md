## Key takeaways

When a computer doesn't recognize a phone over USB, the cause usually starts with the cable, the lock screen, or a permission prompt. If the phone charges but no files show up, suspect the cable first. Apple says the cable must support both data and charging.

An iPhone needs to be unlocked, and you need to tap Trust on the "Trust This Computer?" alert before the computer can access your photos. On a Windows PC, you use the Apple Devices app.

An Android phone opens a file window on the computer only after you change its USB setting to File Transfer in the notification.

"My phone charges, but nothing shows up on the computer." "It used to work, and today my PC won't see my phone." This is a common place to get stuck when you try to move photos to a computer. This guide covers only steps you can check in Apple and Google documentation. It doesn't test or recommend any cable or program.

## The phone charges but files don't show up: is it the cable?

It can be. [Apple's guide for when your computer doesn't recognize your iPhone](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108643) says the cable must support both data and charging. [Google's file transfer guide](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9064445?hl=en) also notes that not all USB cables can transfer files. If you've only used a cable for charging, try a different one.

Combining the two guides, check in this order. Once the phone is recognized, skip the rest.

1. Unplug the cable and plug it back in.
2. See whether the phone chimes or vibrates when you connect it. If it doesn't, unplug the cable and check the charging port for debris.
3. Switch to another cable that supports data transfer.
4. Plug into another USB port on the computer.
5. Connect to a different computer.

Google explains that connecting your phone to a different computer tests the phone's port, and connecting a different device to your computer tests the computer's port. If the phone works on another computer but not yours, suspect your computer's port or settings.

## What if a Windows PC doesn't recognize your iPhone?

On a Windows PC, you check the iPhone in the Apple Devices app. These steps follow [Apple's guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108643).

1. Open the Apple Devices app.
2. Connect the iPhone to the Windows PC with a USB cable.
3. Unlock the iPhone.
4. If the "Trust This Computer?" alert appears on the iPhone, tap Trust.
5. See whether the iPhone appears in the sidebar of the Apple Devices app.

If it still doesn't appear, Apple says to check these items in order.

| Check | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Cable and connection | Follow the cable and port steps above |
| Software conflicts | See whether other software, such as security software, is blocking the connection |
| Windows version | Make sure you're using the latest version of Windows |
| iOS version | Make sure you're using the latest version of iOS |

According to [Apple's guide to allowing accessories to connect](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111806), an iPhone by default needs to be unlocked before it connects to a computer. If you don't unlock it, it won't communicate with the computer and might not charge. If it still isn't recognized after you unlock it, disconnect the cable, unlock the iPhone, and reconnect.

## What if your Mac doesn't recognize your iPhone?

On a Mac, the iPhone should appear in the Finder sidebar. These steps follow [Apple's guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108643).

1. Connect the iPhone to the Mac with a USB cable.
2. If the "Allow accessory to connect?" alert appears on the Mac, click Allow.
3. If the "Trust This Computer?" alert appears on the iPhone, tap Trust.
4. See whether the iPhone appears in the Finder sidebar.

If the Finder window has no sidebar, choose View > Show Sidebar from the menu bar. If the iPhone still doesn't appear, check the cable and port, and see whether a VPN or other third-party security software is blocking the connection. Also make sure macOS and iOS are up to date.

If you clicked Don't Allow on the Mac's accessory prompt, reconnect and allow it as described in [Apple's guide to allowing accessories on a Mac](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102282).

## What if the "Trust This Computer" alert doesn't appear?

According to [Apple's guide to the Trust This Computer alert](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109054), a computer you choose not to trust can't access content on your iPhone, and the alert appears every time you connect to it. If the alert never appears, or you tapped Don't Trust by mistake, reset your location and privacy settings.

1. Open Settings.
2. Tap General.
3. Tap Transfer or Reset [Device].
4. Tap Reset.
5. Tap Reset Location & Privacy.

After the reset, the iPhone asks again whether to trust computers you trusted before. If you tap Trust but the computer doesn't accept your answer, Apple suggests the steps below. Try connecting again after each one.

| Step | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Update macOS or the Apple Devices app on the computer |
| 2 | Disconnect the iPhone and reconnect it |
| 3 | Restart the computer |
| 4 | Restart the iPhone |
| 5 | Reset Location & Privacy |
| 6 | Reset Network Settings |

Resetting network settings also resets Wi-Fi networks and passwords, cellular settings, and VPN and APN settings. Make a note of your Wi-Fi password first.

## What if your PC doesn't recognize your Android phone?

An Android phone may only charge when you plug it in. These steps for a Windows computer follow [Google's file transfer guide](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9064445?hl=en).

1. Unlock the phone.
2. Connect the phone to the computer with a USB cable.
3. On the phone, tap the "Charging this device via USB" notification.
4. Under "Use USB for," select File Transfer.
5. When a file transfer window opens on the computer, drag files.
6. When you're done, eject the phone from Windows and unplug the cable.

Google notes that some of these steps only work on Android 14 and up. Menu names may differ by manufacturer and version.

If the file window still doesn't open, Google suggests checking these items.

| Where to check | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Computer | Make sure Windows automatically detects new hardware. Restart the computer |
| Phone | Update your Android version. Restart the phone |
| USB connection | Try a different cable. Test the ports with another computer and another device |

This Google guide covers Windows and Chromebook only. For using an Android phone with a Mac, check your phone manufacturer's instructions.

## How do you import iPhone photos once it's recognized?

Once the iPhone appears, import your photos. According to [Apple's photo transfer guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120267), a Mac uses the Import screen in the Photos app. On a Windows PC, you install the Apple Devices app from the Microsoft Store and import with the Microsoft Photos app.

| Situation | Apple's guidance |
| --- | --- |
| iCloud Photos is on | Download the original, full-resolution photos to the iPhone before you import to a PC |
| Photos don't open well on Windows | In Settings > Camera > Formats, choose Most Compatible. From then on, photos and videos are captured as JPEG and H.264 |
| Importing to a Mac | Click the device name in the Photos sidebar, and disconnect after the import finishes |

Most Compatible applies only to photos you take afterward. It doesn't change the format of photos you already took. Detailed steps for a Mac and a Windows PC are in [Best way to save photos from iPhone without iCloud](/blog/en/iphone-photo-backup).

## What if the computer still doesn't recognize your phone?

If you've changed the cable, the port, and even the computer and it still doesn't work, suspect the phone's port or hardware. Apple says you might need to contact support for service. For an Android phone, check the manufacturer's support first.

## Computer not recognizing phone FAQ

### Why does my phone charge but not show up on the computer?

The cable may support charging only, or on Android the USB setting may be set to charging. Apple says to use a cable that supports both data and charging. On Android, choose File Transfer in the notification.

### I tapped Don't Trust by mistake.

Go to Settings > General > Transfer or Reset [Device] > Reset > Reset Location & Privacy. When you reconnect, the Trust alert appears again.

### Do I need an app to see my iPhone on a Windows PC?

Apple's guide says to open the Apple Devices app on the Windows PC and connect the iPhone. To import photos, Apple also says to install the Apple Devices app from the Microsoft Store first.

### Will a computer recognize my iPhone if it's locked?

According to Apple, an iPhone by default needs to be unlocked before it connects to a computer. Unlock it and reconnect.

### I don't see a File Transfer option on Android.

Google says some steps only work on Android 14 and up. Update Android and restart the phone. If the menu names are different, check your manufacturer's instructions.
