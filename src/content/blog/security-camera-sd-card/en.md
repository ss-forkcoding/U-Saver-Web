## Key takeaways

If you use a security camera with an SD card, pick the card by the conditions the camera maker sets: maximum capacity, speed class, and file system. Even within one brand, the maximum capacity differs by model and hardware version.

When the card fills up, a home security camera overwrites the oldest footage first. Continuous recording writes and erases the card nonstop, so camera makers recommend high endurance cards.

A card is a consumable. Test it right after you buy it, and plan to replace it every few years.

"Can I put any microSD card in my security camera?" "I inserted an SD card, but nothing is recording." These questions come up a lot when people set up a home camera. This guide covers what to check when you choose a security camera SD card and how to look after it. This guide doesn't recommend any product, and we didn't test any cards or cameras. The manufacturer documents below are used only as examples of how to read them.

## Do you need an SD card in a security camera?

You need one if you want recordings stored inside the camera. [Reolink's guide](https://support.reolink.com/hc/en-us/articles/360005143453) says its devices don't come with a microSD card unless you buy one with them. It also says you don't need a card if you don't need recordings.

[TP-Link's guide](https://www.tp-link.com/us/support/faq/3048/) says a microSD card gives you local storage for recordings without the cost of a cloud subscription.

Some models don't take a card at all. [Wyze's support article](https://support.wyze.com/hc/en-us/articles/46547872366107-What-is-the-microSD-card-size-limit-for-my-Wyze-Cam) says the Wyze Video Doorbell v1 and Video Doorbell Pro aren't compatible with microSD cards. Check for a slot before you buy a card.

## What should you check first on a security camera SD card?

Look at the memory card section of the camera manual or the maker's product page. Here's what three manufacturers say.

| What to check | TP-Link (Tapo, Kasa) | Reolink | Wyze |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Card type | microSDHC, microSDXC | SD, SDHC, SDXC (microSD) | microSD |
| Maximum capacity | Tapo 512GB, Kasa 256GB (varies by model) | 64GB to 512GB (varies by model) | 32GB, 256GB, or 512GB (varies by model) |
| Speed class | Class 10, U1, V10 or higher | Class 10 or higher; V30 or higher recommended for 4K | Check your model's article |
| File system | Formatted by the camera | FAT32 | Check your model's article |

These are the general requirements in each maker's documents. Confirm the values for your model on its product page.

## How do you find the maximum SD card capacity for a security camera?

Look it up by model name and hardware version. Products with the same name can have different limits by hardware version.

| Manufacturer document | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| [Reolink](https://support.reolink.com/hc/en-us/articles/360005143453) | Argus 2 and Argus Pro 64GB, Argus 3 128GB, Argus 4 512GB. The RLC series ranges from 64GB to 128GB to 512GB by hardware |
| [Wyze](https://support.wyze.com/hc/en-us/articles/46547872366107-What-is-the-microSD-card-size-limit-for-my-Wyze-Cam) | Cam v1/v2 32GB, Cam v3 and v4 256GB, Cam Pan v4 512GB |
| [TP-Link](https://www.tp-link.com/us/support/faq/3048/) | Tapo cameras and hubs 8GB to 512GB, Kasa cameras 8GB to 256GB |

Reolink warns that a card above the supported capacity may have compatibility issues and the camera may not record normally. It says the PoE camera models on sale have been tested with 512GB cards, and other models are officially guaranteed only up to 256GB.

Capacity also ties to the card standard. As Reolink notes, SDHC only goes up to 32GB. Cards above 32GB are SDXC. The difference is explained in [SDHC Card vs SDXC vs SDUC](/blog/en/sdhc-vs-sdxc).

## What speed class does a security camera SD card need?

Pick the minimum class the maker lists, or higher. TP-Link says cards below Class 10 (U1/V10) may not support continuous video recording reliably. Reolink recommends V30 or higher for 4K recording.

The number in a speed class marks minimum write speed. Reolink's guide also explains that V10 means a minimum of 10 MB/s and V30 a minimum of 30 MB/s. How to read these markings is in [Class 10 SD Card, U3, and V30](/blog/en/sd-card-speed-class).

## Does a security camera need a high endurance SD card?

Consider one for continuous recording. TP-Link strongly recommends High Endurance, Max Endurance, or Pro Endurance cards for continuous recording. It also says regular cards designed for digital cameras aren't recommended for home security cameras or monitoring.

The reason is the write limit of flash memory. TP-Link's guide says each write operation gradually wears down the flash chips.

High endurance cards can still fail. How to read endurance figures, and why makers rate them differently, is in [High Endurance SD Card](/blog/en/high-endurance-sd-card).

## How long does a security camera SD card record?

It depends on resolution and capacity. Here's part of the continuous recording estimate in [TP-Link's guide](https://www.tp-link.com/us/support/faq/3048/) (all at 15fps).

| Resolution | 64GB | 128GB | 256GB |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2MP (1920x1080) | 192 hours | 384 hours | 768 hours |
| 4MP (2556x1440) | 96 hours | 192 hours | 384 hours |
| 8MP (3840x2160) | 48 hours | 96 hours | 192 hours |

TP-Link says these figures are based on average video bitrate measured in lab conditions. Actual time varies with scene complexity, the share of motion, and frame rate. Don't apply this table to other brands' cameras.

## What happens when the security camera SD card is full?

With loop recording on, the camera overwrites the oldest footage first. TP-Link's guide says that when loop recording is enabled, the camera automatically overwrites the oldest footage once the card is full, so recording never stops.

So copy off any footage you want to keep before it's overwritten. Reolink notes that not every type of recording is saved to the microSD card. Check in your camera app which recordings stay on the card.

## How do you choose and install a security camera SD card?

1. Check that the camera has a microSD slot.
2. Find the maximum capacity for your model and hardware version on the product page.
3. Check the required speed class and pick that class or higher.
4. For continuous recording, consider a high endurance card. For card sizes, see [Micro SD Card and SD Card](/blog/en/microsd-vs-sd).
5. Buy from an authorized seller. TP-Link says to avoid cheap, used, or refurbished cards.
6. Insert the card and format it in the camera app. Reolink's guide says to format the card in the camera and notes that formatting erases all data on the card.
7. Test recording and playback right after you buy. TP-Link recommends testing the card immediately after purchase.

For an outdoor camera, also check the operating temperature range in the card's specs. TP-Link's guide asks you to look at it.

## What if the security camera doesn't recognize the SD card?

First suspect a fake-capacity card. TP-Link explains that the camera formats the card's entire storage space, so a card with falsified capacity fails to format correctly and won't work. It names counterfeit cards as a leading cause of unrecognized cards, formatting failures, and failures to overwrite recordings.

| Symptom | Check first |
| --- | --- |
| Card isn't recognized | Over the maximum capacity, fake card |
| Formatting fails | Fake card, damaged card |
| Recording stops or doesn't start | Speed class too low, capacity over the limit |

How to spot a fake card is in [Fake SD Card](/blog/en/fake-sd-card), and general troubleshooting is in [SD Card Corrupted or Not Recognized?](/blog/en/sd-card-not-working)

## When should you replace a security camera SD card?

Cards have a limited read and write life. TP-Link recommends planning to replace your card every few years to reduce the risk of failure. For an exact interval, check the camera and card makers' guidance.

Consider replacing it in these cases.

1. The camera app reports a card error.
2. Recording still stops or won't play back after you format the card in the camera.
3. A computer doesn't recognize the card.

Dash cams also record and re-record nonstop. Compare how their cards are managed in [Micro SD Card for Dash Cam](/blog/en/dashcam-sd-card).

## How do you move security camera footage to an iPhone?

iPhone has no card slot, so you connect the microSD card through a card reader. [Apple's guide to external storage](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/external-storage-devices-iph95baac91f/ios) lists formats iPhone can use, including exFAT and FAT32. FAT32, which Reolink requires, is on that list.

Check your camera app's instructions before you remove the card. While the card is out, the camera can't record to it. Connection steps are in [Micro SD for iPhone](/blog/en/iphone-microsd), and importing to the Photos app is in [How to Use an iPhone SD Card Reader](/blog/en/iphone-sd-card-reader).

## Security camera SD card FAQ

### Can I put a 1TB card in a security camera?

Only within the limit your camera supports. The camera limits checked for this guide go up to 512GB. Reolink lists 1TB for hub devices like the Home Hub, but hub and camera limits are different.

### Can I use a microSD card from my phone in a security camera?

Yes, if its capacity and speed class meet the camera's requirements. Move the files off the card first, then format it in the camera. Formatting erases your phone's files.

### Can I format a security camera SD card as exFAT?

Follow the format your camera requires. Reolink requires FAT32. If the format doesn't match, reformat the card in the camera app. Steps for a computer are in [How to Reformat a Micro SD Card](/blog/en/format-microsd).

### Does a high endurance card never need replacing?

No. High endurance cards also have a write limit. TP-Link says it can't guarantee 100% compatibility with every card because card quality varies widely.

### Can I share a card between a security camera and a dash cam?

Yes, if it meets both devices' requirements. Format it in the new device each time you switch. Dash cam requirements are in [Micro SD Card for Dash Cam](/blog/en/dashcam-sd-card).
