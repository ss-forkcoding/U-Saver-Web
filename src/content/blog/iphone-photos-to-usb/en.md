## Key takeaways

You can transfer photos from an iPhone to a USB drive without a computer. Select the photos in the Photos app, choose "Export Unmodified Originals," and save them to the drive.

An iPhone with a USB-C port takes a USB-C flash drive directly. An iPhone with a Lightning port connects through a camera adapter.

To move videos larger than 4GB, the drive needs to be formatted as exFAT. The FAT32 format can't store files over 4GB.

Before deleting anything from your iPhone, confirm that the photos on the drive actually open.

"My storage is full and I don't have a computer." "Will photos lose quality on a USB drive?" "Which flash drive should I buy?" These are the questions people ask most when they want to move iPhone photos to USB.

To transfer photos from iPhone to USB, you need the iPhone, a flash drive, and in some cases one adapter. This guide covers connecting the drive, saving the photos, and checking the result.

## What do you need to transfer photos from iPhone to USB?

To transfer photos from iPhone to USB, you need a flash drive or adapter that fits your iPhone's port. iPhone 15 and later use USB-C, and earlier models use Lightning.

| iPhone port | How to connect | What else you need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| USB-C | Plug in a USB-C flash drive directly | A USB-C to USB-A adapter if your drive is USB-A |
| Lightning | Plug the flash drive into a camera adapter | Lightning to USB Camera Adapter or Lightning to USB 3 Camera Adapter |

If you're buying a new flash drive for a USB-C iPhone, a drive with a USB-C connector works with no adapter. For a Lightning iPhone, plan on having a camera adapter whichever flash drive you choose.

External hard drives often need outside power. [Apple's guide to external storage](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/external-storage-devices-iph95baac91f/ios) recommends a powered hub for USB-C iPhones, and a Lightning to USB 3 Camera Adapter with a USB power adapter for Lightning iPhones.

## How to save iPhone photos to a flash drive in 3 steps

### Step 1. Connect the drive and find it in the Files app

Plug in the drive, open the Files app, and go to the Browse screen. If the drive's name appears under Locations, your iPhone has recognized it.

If the name doesn't appear, check the drive's format and power first. According to Apple, an iPhone reads storage devices that have a single data partition.

### Step 2. Choose "Export Unmodified Originals" in Photos

Select the photos and videos you want in the Photos app, then tap the Share button. In the Share menu, choose ["Export Unmodified Originals."](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111762)

"Export Unmodified Originals" saves the original file as it was captured, so there's no loss in quality. Photos you edited in the Photos app are saved in their pre-edit form, so check any photo where the edit matters.

### Step 3. Select the USB drive and save

When the Files window opens, pick your drive under Locations and tap Save. Unplugging the drive before saving finishes can leave some files behind.

If you have a lot of photos, move them by month or year. Smaller batches make it easier to see how far you got if saving stops partway.

## Which USB format should you use for iPhone photos?

exFAT is the safest format for moving iPhone photos to USB. exFAT stores videos over 4GB and can be read on Windows PCs and Macs.

| USB format | Works with iPhone | Files over 4GB |
| --- | --- | --- |
| exFAT | Yes | Can be stored |
| FAT32 | Yes | Can't be stored |
| NTFS | Not on Apple's supported list | Not applicable |

Choose exFAT if you shoot a lot of video. FAT32 is also fine if you're only moving photos. A flash drive formatted as NTFS on a Windows PC needs to be reformatted.

Reformatting erases everything on the drive. If the drive already holds files, copy them somewhere else first.

## Four checks before deleting photos from your iPhone

Don't delete photos from your iPhone unless you can confirm all four of these.

- The photos open when you browse the drive in the Files app.
- A long video plays through to the end.
- The drive has at least as many files as photos you selected. A Live Photo is saved as separate photo and video files, so the file count goes up.
- Important photos also exist somewhere other than this drive. If a single flash drive fails, the photos on it can be lost.

If iCloud Photos is on, deleting a photo from your iPhone also removes it from other devices signed in to the same Apple Account. If your goal is to free up space, check your iCloud Photos setting before you delete anything.

## FAQ: transferring photos from iPhone to USB

### Does transferring photos from iPhone to USB reduce quality?

No, not with "Export Unmodified Originals." That option saves the original captured file to the drive.

### Can you transfer photos from iPhone to USB without a computer?

Yes. Connect the drive to your iPhone and use only the Photos and Files apps. For other kinds of data, see [how to back up an iPhone without a computer](/blog/en/backup-iphone-without-computer).

### Why can't I see my iPhone photos in the Files app?

iPhone photos live in the Photos app, not the Files app. [Apple's guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102570) also points to the Photos app for your photo library. Photos need to be exported to USB from the Photos app.

### What if videos over 4GB won't save when I transfer from iPhone to USB?

Reformat the drive as exFAT. The FAT32 format can't store files over 4GB. Reformatting erases the drive, so copy its contents first.

### Can I bring photos back from USB to my iPhone?

Yes. Select the photos on the drive in the Files app, then choose Save Image from the Share menu to save them to Photos.
