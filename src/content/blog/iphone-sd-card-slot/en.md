## Key takeaways

You can't plug an SD card into an iPhone directly. There's no card slot, so you put the card in a card reader and connect the reader to the iPhone's charging port.

An iPhone with USB-C uses the USB-C to SD Card Camera Reader. A Lightning iPhone uses the Lightning to SD Card Camera Reader. Both names come from Apple's guide.

Once connected, the card opens in the Files app, and the Photos app can import photos and videos from it or export them to it.

"I want to see my camera's photos on my iPhone right away." "Is there a card slot on the side of the iPhone?" These are the questions people ask when they try to plug an SD card into an iPhone. A reader does the job a slot would.

## Does the iPhone have an SD card slot?

No. For example, the external buttons and connectors in the [iPhone 15 tech specs](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111831) list a USB-C connector and a SIM tray, with no card slot.

[Apple's guide to external storage](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/external-storage-devices-iph95baac91f/ios) says to attach an SD card reader to the charging port with a compatible connector or adapter. That's the way to use an SD card with an iPhone.

## Which SD card reader does your iPhone need?

Check your iPhone's port first. According to [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105099), iPhone 15 and later use USB-C. Earlier models use Lightning.

| iPhone port | Reader named in Apple's guide | Sold separately |
| --- | --- | --- |
| USB-C | USB-C to SD Card Camera Reader | Yes |
| Lightning | Lightning to SD Card Camera Reader | Yes |

Apple's USB-C guide lists "SD cards using SD card adapters" among the devices you can connect. We haven't tested third-party card readers. If you use one, check that the card appears in the Files app after you connect it.

The adapters for USB drives are covered in [iPhone OTG USB: which adapter do you need?](/blog/en/iphone-otg-usb)

## Which formats does the SD card need?

An iPhone reads storage formatted as exFAT, FAT32, FAT, APFS, or macOS Extended (HFS+), with a single data partition. The source is [Apple's guide to external storage](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/external-storage-devices-iph95baac91f/ios).

Each SD capacity standard comes with a set file system, according to the [SD Association](https://www.sdcard.org/developers/sd-standard-overview/capacity-sd-sdhc-sdxc-sduc/).

| Standard | Capacity | File system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SD | Up to 2 GB | FAT12, FAT16 |
| SDHC | Over 2 GB up to 32 GB | FAT32 |
| SDXC | Over 32 GB up to 2 TB | exFAT |
| SDUC | Over 2 TB up to 128 TB | exFAT |

FAT32 can't store a file larger than 4 GB. Use an exFAT card to move long videos. The differences between formats are in [how to format a USB drive to exFAT](/blog/en/format-usb-exfat).

## How to import photos from an SD card to your iPhone

These steps follow [Apple's guide to importing photos](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/import-and-export-photos-and-videos-iph480caa1f3/ios).

1. Insert the card reader into the Lightning or USB-C connector on your iPhone.
2. Insert the SD card into the reader. It fits only one way, so don't force it.
3. Open the Photos app.
4. Tap Import.
5. Select the photos and videos you want, then select your import destination.
6. Tap Import All, or tap the items you want, tap Import, then tap Import Selected.
7. After the import, choose whether to keep or delete the photos on the card, then disconnect the reader.

## How to export iPhone photos to an SD card

The same Apple guide explains how to export photos and videos to a connected memory card.

1. Connect the reader with the card to your iPhone.
2. In the Photos app, select the photos and videos to export.
3. Tap the Share button, then tap Export Unmodified Original.
4. Tap the card below Locations, then tap Save.

For edited photos, the unmodified original is exported. Items in the Hidden album aren't transferred while the album is locked. The same steps for a USB drive are in [how to transfer photos from iPhone to USB](/blog/en/iphone-photos-to-usb).

## How do you see the card's files in the Files app?

In the Files app, tap Browse, then tap the card's name below Locations. If you don't see Locations, tap Browse again. When you're done, remove the reader from the charging port.

If the card's name doesn't appear, check these in order.

| Order | What to check | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Card direction | Remove the card and insert it the right way |
| 2 | Card format | Check the format on a Mac or PC. NTFS isn't on Apple's list |
| 3 | Partitions | Make sure there's a single data partition |
| 4 | Reader | Reconnect with a reader named in Apple's guide or another reader |

## FAQ: plug an SD card into an iPhone

### Can I plug an SD card straight into my iPhone?

No. The iPhone has no SD card slot, so you need a card reader connected to the charging port.

### Which SD card reader should I buy?

Pick one that matches your iPhone's port. Apple's guide names the USB-C to SD Card Camera Reader and the Lightning to SD Card Camera Reader. We haven't tested or recommended any specific third-party product.

### Can my iPhone use an SD card of 64 GB or more?

According to the SD Association, SDXC cards over 32 GB use exFAT. exFAT is on Apple's list of external storage formats.

### Does importing photos delete them from the SD card?

According to Apple, you choose whether to keep or delete the photos on the card after the import finishes.

### Is the content of an SD card encrypted?

In the SD Association table, the default format for SDXC cards is exFAT. [Microsoft's comparison table](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/fileio/filesystem-functionality-comparison) lists no file system level encryption for exFAT. If you lose the card, someone else can read it, so store it carefully.
