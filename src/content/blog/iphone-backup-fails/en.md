## Key takeaways

When an iPhone is not backing up, the cause is usually storage, Wi-Fi, or a pending software update. Check the message on screen first, then follow the steps for that message.

If iCloud storage is short, check Next Backup Size. Turn off backups for apps you don't use, or move photos elsewhere to make the backup smaller.

If a computer backup fails, update both the computer and the iPhone and check the cable. If you see a message about disk space, free up space on the computer.

"It says there isn't enough iCloud storage to back up." "I keep getting an alert that my last backup couldn't be completed." "The backup option is gray and I can't tap it." These are common complaints when an iPhone won't back up. This guide lists the cause and fix for each message, following Apple's support articles.

## iPhone not backing up: which message do you see?

[Apple's guide for iCloud backup problems](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102563) and [its guide for computer backup problems](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108346) sort the issues like this.

| What you see | What to do first |
| --- | --- |
| Alert that there's not enough iCloud storage | Check Next Backup Size, then make the backup smaller or upgrade to iCloud+ |
| Message that your last backup couldn't be completed | Check Wi-Fi, software updates, another Wi-Fi network, and iCloud storage |
| iCloud backup takes a long time | Check your upload speed and leave the iPhone on power and Wi-Fi for 24 hours |
| iCloud Backup option is grayed out | Check whether a restore is in progress or a profile restricts backups |
| Computer says an error occurred | Update the iPhone to the latest iOS |
| Computer says there's not enough disk space | Free up space on the Mac or Windows PC |

## iPhone not backing up because iCloud storage is full

According to [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108922), you get 5 GB of iCloud storage for free when you set up iCloud. If you run out, your device won't back up to iCloud. Check how much space the backup needs.

1. Open the Settings app and tap your name.
2. Tap iCloud. You can see how much iCloud storage you've used at the top.
3. Tap Storage or Manage Account Storage.
4. Tap Backups, then tap the device you're using.
5. Check Next Backup Size. It might take a few minutes to calculate.

If the backup needs more space than you have, you have two options. Delete information you don't need to make the backup smaller, or upgrade your iCloud+ plan.

## How do you make an iPhone backup smaller?

[Apple's guide to managing iCloud storage](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108922) describes three ways.

| Method | Steps | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turn off app backups | Settings > your name > iCloud > Storage or Manage Account Storage > Backups > device name, then turn off apps | Turning off an app removes its information from iCloud. Some apps can't be turned off |
| Delete old backups | On the same Backups screen, choose a device and tap Turn Off and Delete from iCloud | Deleting a backup also turns off iCloud Backup for that device |
| Shrink the photo library | Save photos and videos somewhere else, such as a computer, then back up | Your photo library is part of the backup only if you don't use iCloud Photos |

If you don't use iCloud Photos, the device's Backups screen shows how much space Photo Library takes. If that number is large, moving photos first makes the biggest difference. Without a computer, see [the best way to save photos from iPhone without iCloud](/blog/en/iphone-photo-backup) for how to export them to a USB drive.

With iCloud Photos on, deleting a photo on your iPhone also deletes it from iCloud and your other devices on the same Apple Account. Make sure you have a copy somewhere else before you delete anything.

## What if it says your last backup couldn't be completed?

These steps follow [Apple's guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102563). Try backing up again after each step.

1. Open Settings and tap Wi-Fi to make sure you're connected.
2. In Settings, tap General, then Software Update, and install any available updates.
3. Try backing up on another Wi-Fi network.
4. Make sure you have enough iCloud storage for the backup.

After you tap Back Up Now, stay on Wi-Fi until the process ends. According to [Apple's iCloud backup guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108366), the date and time of your last backup appear under Back Up Now. If that date changes, the backup finished.

## iCloud backup taking too long or not running automatically

[Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102563) says to check your Wi-Fi connection and upload speed first. Then leave the iPhone connected to power and Wi-Fi for 24 hours. If the backup still isn't done, make copies of your important data and try the steps again.

If automatic backups don't run, check the conditions in [Apple's iCloud backup guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108366).

| Condition | Where to check |
| --- | --- |
| Back Up This iPhone is on | Settings > your name > iCloud > iCloud Backup |
| Connected to power | Charging cable or charger |
| Connected to Wi-Fi | Settings > Wi-Fi |
| Screen is locked | Screen turned off with the side button |

## iCloud Backup option grayed out

[Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102563) lists two causes.

1. A restore might still be in progress. If Settings > your name > iCloud > iCloud Backup says the device is currently being restored, wait for it to finish. You can create a new backup after the restore ends.
2. A profile might restrict iCloud Backup. Go to Settings > General > VPN & Device Management to see installed profiles. If your iPhone is managed by a company or school, ask the administrator.

## iPhone won't back up to a computer

These steps follow [Apple's guide for computer backup problems](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108346). Try again after each step.

1. Make sure your Mac or Windows PC is up to date. On a Windows PC, get the latest version of the Apple Devices app or iTunes.
2. Connect the iPhone with a USB cable and check that it appears in Finder, iTunes, or the Apple Devices app.
3. Restart the iPhone and the computer.
4. Skip this step on a Mac. On a Windows PC, if iTunes won't respond or the backup never finishes, you might need to update, change, disable, or uninstall your security software.
5. If you have another computer, try backing up there. You can also use iCloud Backup.

If an alert says an error occurred, check that the iPhone has the latest iOS. If it says there's not enough disk space, free up space on the Mac or Windows PC.

## Keep a separate copy of your photos while you fix it

While you work on the backup problem, you can still keep separate copies of photos and contacts. The steps for saving each item to a USB drive without a computer are in [Backup iPhone to USB](/blog/en/backup-iphone-to-usb). The difference between iCloud Backup and a USB copy is in [Back up iPhone without a computer](/blog/en/backup-iphone-without-computer).

If you'll save videos over 4 GB, set up the drive as exFAT. According to [Microsoft's file system comparison](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/fileio/filesystem-functionality-comparison), FAT32 stores files only up to 4 GB. The steps are in [how to format a USB drive to exFAT](/blog/en/format-usb-exfat). To save just your contacts first, see [how to backup contacts on iPhone](/blog/en/iphone-contacts-backup).

A USB copy doesn't replace an iCloud or computer backup. There's no built-in way to move messages or iPhone settings to a USB drive, so fix the original backup problem too.

## FAQ: iPhone not backing up

### What's the most common reason an iPhone won't back up?

The first issue Apple's guide covers is not enough iCloud storage. If Next Backup Size is larger than your available space, the backup can't finish. Check Next Backup Size on the Backups screen first.

### Can I back up without buying more iCloud storage?

You can turn off backups for apps you don't use or delete old backups to make the backup smaller. If you don't use iCloud Photos, you can move photos elsewhere before backing up. If you have a computer, you can back up to it instead.

### Does turning off an app's backup delete its data from my iPhone?

Apple says turning off an app's backup removes that app's information from iCloud. Apple doesn't say it deletes anything from the iPhone. The app's information just won't be in future iCloud backups.

### What if my computer doesn't find my iPhone?

If your iPhone shows "Trust this Computer?", tap Trust. According to [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108643), the cable must support both data and charging. Try another USB cable, another port, or another computer.

### If I copy photos to a USB drive, can I turn off iCloud Backup?

A USB drive holds copies of individual items such as photos, videos, and contacts. To bring back messages and iPhone settings, keep using iCloud Backup or a computer backup.
