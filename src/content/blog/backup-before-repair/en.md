## Key takeaways

Back up your iPhone before factory reset or repair. A factory reset erases everything, and Apple says a device returned from service might have been erased.

Use iCloud or a computer for the backup. A separate copy of your photos and contacts on a USB drive stays safe even if the backup fails.

Before you erase, check that the date of your last backup changed to today.

"I'm only replacing the battery. Do I really need a backup?" "Where do my photos go after a reset?" These questions come up before every repair or reset. Here's how to back up iPhone before factory reset or service, based on Apple's guides.

## Why back up iPhone before factory reset or repair?

[Apple's service preparation guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109519) lists backing up as the first step before service. The same guide says your device might have been erased when you get it back. In that case you restore it from an iCloud or computer backup, or set it up as new.

A battery replacement is a service visit, so the same steps apply. Your iPhone may come back unerased, but you can't know in advance. Without a backup, anything that existed only on the iPhone can't be brought back.

| Situation | Data on the iPhone | What to do first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repair, including battery replacement | Might be erased | Back up, check syncing, set up Find My |
| Factory reset | Erased | Finish a backup and check its date |
| Selling or giving away | Erased | Back up, transfer to the new device, erase |

The selling steps follow [Apple's guide for selling or trading in](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109511).

## Which backup method should you use before a reset or repair?

| Method | What it holds | What you need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| iCloud Backup | Information and settings that don't already sync to iCloud | Wi-Fi and enough iCloud storage |
| Computer backup | Information and settings on the device. Health data only in an encrypted backup | A Mac or Windows PC and a cable |
| USB copy | Item-by-item files such as photos, videos, and contacts | An exFAT or FAT32 USB drive |

According to [Apple's guide to what iCloud backs up](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108770), photos aren't included in iCloud Backup when iCloud Photos is on, because they sync to iCloud. The same goes for messages when Messages in iCloud is on. You check those synced items in the "check syncing" step below.

## How to back up with iCloud

These steps follow [Apple's iCloud Backup guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108366).

1. Connect your iPhone to Wi-Fi.
2. Open the Settings app, tap your name, then tap iCloud.
3. Tap iCloud Backup.
4. Tap Back Up Now.
5. Stay connected to Wi-Fi until the process ends.
6. Check that the date and time under Back Up Now changed.

If you get an alert that iCloud storage is full, see [iPhone not backing up?](/blog/en/iphone-backup-fails) for how to shrink the backup.

## How to back up with a computer

On a Mac, use the Finder. These steps follow [Apple's Mac backup guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108796).

1. Connect your iPhone to the Mac with a cable.
2. Open the Finder and select your iPhone in the sidebar.
3. If asked, trust the computer and enter your passcode.
4. To save Health and Activity data, select "Encrypt local backup" and create a password.
5. Click Back Up Now.
6. When it finishes, check that the date and time of your last backup appear.

If you forget the password for an encrypted backup, you can't use that backup. Write it down somewhere safe.

## What else should you do before sending your iPhone in for repair?

After the backup, [Apple's service preparation guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109519) lists these steps.

1. Go to Settings > your name > iCloud and check that the status icon next to Saved to iCloud is green.
2. Turn off Apple Cash in Settings > Wallet & Apple Pay.
3. Remove your cards and passes in the Wallet app.
4. Check Parts and Service History. If a part shows Finish Repair, finish that repair first.
5. Set up Find My for the repair, as shown below.

| iOS version | Find My step |
| --- | --- |
| iOS 27 or later | In the Find My app, select the device and turn on Repair or Trade In |
| iOS 26 or earlier | Go to Settings > your name > Find My and turn off Find My iPhone |

According to [Apple's Ready for Repair or Trade In guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/122189), on iOS 27 or later Find My stays on during an eligible repair. It turns off automatically when the technician marks the repair complete. On iOS 26 or earlier, Apple might not be able to service your iPhone if Find My is still on.

## How to factory reset after your backup

[Apple's factory reset guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108931) also starts with a backup. The reset follows these steps.

1. Go to Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone.
2. Tap Erase All Content and Settings, then tap Continue.
3. Choose to keep or delete your eSIM. If you delete it, contact your carrier to reactivate your plan.
4. Enter your passcode and wait for your backup to finish.
5. Enter your Apple Account password.
6. When the reset completes, the Hello screen appears.

Before step 2, look again at the date of your last iCloud or computer backup. Once the reset finishes, nothing stays on the iPhone.

## What to do when you get your iPhone back

[Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109519) says that if the device was erased, you restore it from a backup or set it up as new. The steps for a computer backup are in [Apple's guide to transferring from a computer backup](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120001).

If you set it up as new and later want your old iCloud backup, erase the device and restore from that backup as soon as possible. New iCloud backups can overwrite the older ones. If you turned off Find My for the repair, turn it back on.

## Why keep a USB copy of your photos and contacts?

A slow or failing backup may not finish before your repair appointment. A USB copy of your photos, videos, and contacts stays separate from that backup. The item-by-item steps are in [Backup iPhone to USB](/blog/en/backup-iphone-to-usb), and saving a contacts file is covered in [How to backup contacts on iPhone](/blog/en/iphone-contacts-backup).

If you'll copy videos over 4 GB, prepare the drive as exFAT. [Microsoft's file system comparison](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/fileio/filesystem-functionality-comparison) shows FAT32 stores files up to 4 GB. The steps are in [How to format a USB drive to exFAT](/blog/en/format-usb-exfat).

A USB copy doesn't replace an iCloud or computer backup. There's no built-in way to move messages, app data, or iPhone settings to a USB drive. The differences are compared in [Back up iPhone without a computer](/blog/en/backup-iphone-without-computer).

## FAQ: back up iPhone before factory reset or repair

### Does a battery replacement always erase my iPhone?

Apple only says the device might have been erased. That doesn't mean it always is. You can't know in advance, so back up before you hand it over.

### What if my iPhone is unresponsive and I can't back up?

Apple says to complete as many steps as you can. You can handle Find My from the Find My app on another device or at icloud.com/find. If you have an earlier iCloud backup, check its date.

### I use iCloud Photos. Do I still need to back up?

With iCloud Photos on, photos sync to iCloud and aren't part of iCloud Backup. Before service, check that the sync status in Settings > your name > iCloud is green, and still make a backup for everything else.

### If I turn off iCloud Backup, is my old backup deleted right away?

According to [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108770), backups stored in iCloud are kept for 180 days after you turn off iCloud Backup for a device, then deleted.

### Is copying photos to USB enough before a factory reset?

A USB drive only holds item-by-item copies such as photos, videos, and contacts. To bring back messages and iPhone settings, make an iCloud or computer backup as well.
