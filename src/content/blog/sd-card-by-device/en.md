## Key takeaways

Every device has its own rules for a micro SD card. Before you buy one, check your device's manual or official support page for the card size, maximum capacity, speed marks, and format.

Even among microSD cards, devices use them differently. Nintendo Switch 2 only uses microSD Express cards for game data, and the 3DS family only goes up to 32 GB. Steam Deck formats cards as ext4.

An iPhone has no card slot. You connect a card reader to the charging port and use the card in the Files and Photos apps.

"Can I move the card from my phone to my Switch?" "What makes a camera card different?" Questions like these come up once you use a micro SD card in more than one device. This guide puts the device rules on one page and links to a detailed guide for each device. It doesn't recommend specific products, and we didn't test any cards.

## How do you read the marks on a micro SD card?

A card shows its capacity standard and speed marks. Match them against the marks in your device's manual.

Each capacity standard has a set capacity range and default file system, according to the [SD Association's capacity overview](https://www.sdcard.org/developers/sd-standard-overview/capacity-sd-sdhc-sdxc-sduc/).

| Standard | Capacity | Default file system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SD (SDSC) | Up to 2 GB | FAT12, FAT16 |
| SDHC | Over 2 GB up to 32 GB | FAT32 |
| SDXC | Over 32 GB up to 2 TB | exFAT |
| SDUC | Over 2 TB up to 128 TB | exFAT |

Speed marks matter most for video recording. The [SD Association's speed class page](https://www.sdcard.org/developers/sd-standard-overview/speed-class/) says V30 applies to UHS bus cards, and V60 and V90 apply to UHS-II and UHS-III cards. It tells you to pick a card with the same or a higher class mark than your device asks for.

The same overview lists a microSD card at 11 x 15 x 1.0 mm and an SD card at 32 x 24 x 2.1 mm. For more on the marks, see [SD card speed class](/blog/en/sd-card-speed-class) and [SDHC vs SDXC](/blog/en/sdhc-vs-sdxc). The size difference is covered in [Micro SD card and SD card](/blog/en/microsd-vs-sd).

## How to put a micro SD card in a new device

The steps are similar across devices.

1. Check the card size, maximum capacity, and speed marks in the manual.
2. If the card has files you want to keep, copy them to a computer or another storage device first.
3. If the manual says to power off, do so. Then insert the card into the slot or tray. A card fits only one way, so don't force it.
4. Format the card in the device's settings. Formatting erases everything on the card.
5. Set the card as the save location, or move data to it.
6. To remove the card, unmount it in settings or power off the device first.

Formatting steps differ by device. To format on a computer, see [How to reformat a micro SD card](/blog/en/format-microsd).

## Can you move a card from one device to another?

Some cards move freely and some don't. These examples come from official documentation.

| Situation | What the official documentation says |
| --- | --- |
| A card formatted as internal storage on Android | You can't move it between devices. Format a card you plan to move as portable storage ([Google help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/12153449?hl=en)) |
| A regular microSD from Switch put in Switch 2 | It can only be used to copy screenshots and videos |
| A card used in Steam Deck | It's formatted as ext4. Reformat it in the other device before using it there |
| A card of 64 GB or more put in a 3DS | SDXC and microSDXC cards aren't compatible |

Copy the files on the card to a computer before you put it in another device. If a card won't read, see [SD card not working](/blog/en/sd-card-not-working).

## How do you use a micro SD card with an iPhone?

Connect a card reader to the charging port. [Apple's external storage guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/external-storage-devices-iph95baac91f/ios) says you may need a reader such as the USB-C to SD Card Camera Reader. The card must have a single data partition and be formatted as APFS, APFS (encrypted), macOS Extended (HFS+), exFAT, FAT32, or FAT.

[Apple's camera adapter guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118280) lists SDSC, SDHC, SDXC, miniSD, microSD, and MMC as supported. After you connect the card, open Files, tap Browse, then tap the card's name below Locations.

An ext4 card from Steam Deck uses a format that isn't on Apple's list. Reformat it as exFAT to use it with an iPhone. Reader and app steps are in [SD card reader for iPhone](/blog/en/iphone-sd-card-guide).

## Micro SD card FAQ

### Can I put a micro SD card in any device?

No. Each device has its own rules. The 3DS family only goes up to 32 GB, and Switch 2 only uses microSD Express cards for game data. Check the memory card section of the manual first.

### Can I put a microSD card in a full-size SD slot?

Yes, with an SD adapter. Nintendo's 3DS page also says miniSD and microSD cards work with an SD card adapter. A full-size SD card won't fit in a microSD slot.

### How do I save videos over 4 GB to a card?

Use an exFAT card. In [Microsoft's file system comparison](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/fileio/filesystem-functionality-comparison), the maximum file size on FAT32 is 4 GiB. SDHC cards of 32 GB or less use FAT32 by default.

### Is a card with a higher speed mark always faster?

Only up to what the device supports. Valve lists the Steam Deck card slot as UHS-I. V60 and V90 are marks for UHS-II and faster bus cards.

### Is a micro SD card encrypted?

Microsoft's comparison lists file system level encryption as not supported on exFAT and FAT32. If you lose the card, someone else can read it, so keep it somewhere safe.
