## Key takeaways

Pick an SD card for your camera from the camera's manual, not from a product name. The memory card section lists the card size, capacity standard, and speed class for each video format.

If you only shoot photos, any SD, SDHC, or SDXC card your camera supports will do. Video needs markings such as U3, V30, V60, or V90, depending on resolution and bit rate.

V60 and V90 need UHS-II or faster. If your camera supports only UHS-I, a UHS-II card runs at UHS-I speed.

"What card do I need for 4K video?" "How much capacity should I get?" These are the first questions after buying a camera. This guide covers the markings to check and the order to check them in. It doesn't recommend specific products, and we didn't test any cards. For SD card basics, see [SD card basics](/blog/en/sd-card-basics-guide).

## What should you check first in an SD card for a camera?

Check these five things.

| What to check | Examples | Where to find it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Card size | SD, microSD | Camera manual, card slot |
| Capacity standard | SDHC, SDXC, SDUC | Supported card list in the manual |
| Bus interface | UHS-I, UHS-II, SD Express | Supported card list in the manual |
| Speed class | Class 10, U3, V30, V60, V90 | Card table for each video format |
| Slots and file system | Two slots, exFAT, FAT32 | Memory card notes in the manual |

The [SD Association's capacity page](https://www.sdcard.org/consumers/sd-sdhc-sdxc-and-sduc-card-capacity-choices/) says to confirm compatibility before inserting a card by finding the SD logo and other symbols on the device, or by checking the user manual or manufacturer information.

## How do you read the card requirements in a camera manual?

Manuals list the required card for each shooting mode. For example, the [Sony α7 IV Help Guide](https://helpguide.sony.net/ilc/2110/v1/en/contents/TP1000640149.html) says this.

| Shooting | Maximum bit rate | Required SD card |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Still images | — | SD, SDHC, SDXC |
| XAVC S 4K video | 200Mbps | SDHC/SDXC (U3/V30 or higher) |
| XAVC S HD video | 100Mbps | SDHC/SDXC (U3/V30 or higher) |
| XAVC S-I 4K video | 600Mbps | SDXC V90 or higher |
| XAVC S 4K slow and quick motion | 200Mbps | SDXC V60 or higher |

The same guide says an SDHC/SDXC Class 10 card also works when the bit rate is 60 Mbps or lower. This camera supports UHS-I and UHS-II SD cards, and microSD cards need an adaptor.

This table is for one model. Requirements differ between models, even from the same maker. Follow the table in your own camera's manual.

## What SD card speed does each video quality need?

The [SD Association FAQ](https://www.sdcard.org/consumers/faq/) lists common uses for each speed class.

| Marking | Common use |
| --- | --- |
| Class 6, V6 | SD to HD video recording |
| Class 10, U1, V10 | HD to Full HD video recording |
| U3, V30 | Full HD to 4K video recording |
| V60 | 4K to 8K video recording |
| V90 | 8K video recording |

The [SD Association's speed class page](https://www.sdcard.org/consumers/speed-class-standards-for-video-recording/) explains that video needs a constant write speed, and that speed classes assure minimum sequential performance under defined conditions. The "up to XX MB/s" on a package isn't a guaranteed minimum.

As the Sony example shows, 4K at a higher bit rate needs a higher marking. To read each marking, see [how to read SD card speed classes](/blog/en/sd-card-speed-class).

## Should a camera SD card be SDHC or SDXC?

The SD Association divides capacity standards like this.

| Standard | Capacity | File system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SD | Up to 2GB | FAT12, FAT16 |
| SDHC | More than 2GB, up to 32GB | FAT32 |
| SDXC | More than 32GB, up to 2TB | exFAT |
| SDUC | More than 2TB, up to 128TB | exFAT |

Choose within the standards your camera supports. According to the SD Association, SDXC devices can use SD, SDHC, and SDXC cards. SDXC cards work only in SDXC and SDUC devices. Don't put an SDXC card in a camera that supports only up to SDHC.

The standard also affects video files. The Sony α7 IV guide says long XAVC S recordings on an SDHC card are split into 4 GB files. To record to both slots at once, insert two cards with the same file system. A mix of exFAT and FAT32 cards can't record simultaneously. See [SDHC card vs SDXC vs SDUC](/blog/en/sdhc-vs-sdxc) for the differences.

## Does your camera need a UHS-II SD card?

If your camera asks for V60 or V90, you need a UHS-II or faster card. In the SD Association table, UHS-I mode goes up to V30, and UHS-II and UHS-III modes go up to V90.

| Camera interface | Video markings available |
| --- | --- |
| UHS-I | U1, U3, V6, V10, V30 |
| UHS-II, UHS-III | U1, U3, V6, V10, V30, V60, V90 |
| SD Express | E150, E300, E450, E600 |

If your camera supports only UHS-I, a UHS-II card runs at UHS-I speed. The same goes for copying to a computer: the reader must support UHS-II to reach UHS-II speed. The full combinations are in the [UHS SD card guide](/blog/en/uhs-sd-card).

## How do you choose an SD card for a camera, step by step?

1. Find the memory card section in your camera's manual.
2. Check the card size. A microSD card needs an adapter in an SD slot. See [micro SD card and SD card](/blog/en/microsd-vs-sd).
3. Check the supported capacity standards (SDHC, SDXC).
4. Decide the video format and bit rate you'll use most, and find the matching speed class in the table.
5. If that marking is V60 or V90, check that both camera and card support UHS-II.
6. Choose a card with the same or a higher marking in the same family.
7. If you use two slots together, prepare cards with the same file system.

Buy genuine cards. A card with faked capacity may fail to save your photos. See [how to spot a fake SD card](/blog/en/fake-sd-card).

## How do you prepare a new SD card for a camera?

The SD Association strongly recommends the SD Memory Card Formatter for SD, SDHC, and SDXC cards instead of generic formatting utilities in operating systems. It says generic utilities may result in less than optimal performance. To format inside the camera, follow your camera's manual.

Formatting erases everything on the card. If the card has been used, copy its photos and videos somewhere else first. File system choices and steps are in [how to format an SD card](/blog/en/format-sd-card).

## How do you move camera photos to an iPhone?

An iPhone has no card slot, so you use a card reader. [Apple's camera adapter guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118280) says you can import photos and videos from an SD card with the Apple USB-C to SD Card Reader or the Lightning to SD Card Camera Reader. Supported cards include SDSC, SDHC, SDXC, and microSD.

Connection and import steps are in [how to use an iPhone SD card reader](/blog/en/iphone-sd-card-reader).

## FAQ: SD card for camera

### Do I need a V90 card if I only shoot photos?

It depends on the manual. The Sony α7 IV guide lists SD, SDHC, and SDXC cards for still images and gives speed classes only for video. If you shoot long bursts, check your manual's recommendation.

### Is V30 enough for 4K video?

It depends on the camera and bit rate. The Sony α7 IV lists U3/V30 or higher for 200Mbps XAVC S 4K, but asks for V90 or higher for 600Mbps XAVC S-I 4K.

### Can I use a UHS-II card in a UHS-I camera?

Yes, but it runs at UHS-I speed. In the SD Association table, UHS-I mode goes up to V30.

### Can I use a microSD card in a camera?

If the camera supports it, use it with an adapter. The Sony α7 IV guide says to use the appropriate adaptor with microSD cards.

### Can I use a high endurance card made for dash cams in a camera?

Yes, if it matches the standard and speed class in your camera's manual. For what high endurance means, see [high endurance SD card](/blog/en/high-endurance-sd-card).
