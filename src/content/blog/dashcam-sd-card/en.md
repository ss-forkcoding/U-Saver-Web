## Key takeaways

Choose a micro SD card for your dash cam from the dash cam's manual. The manual lists the card type (microSDHC or microSDXC), capacity range, speed class, and file system. These differ between models, even from the same maker.

When the card fills up, a dash cam deletes the oldest unsaved footage and keeps recording. Treat the card as a consumable: format it regularly and replace it over time. Formatting intervals differ by maker, so follow your manual.

A failing card can still look like it's recording. Play back a recent clip now and then.

"Can I use any micro SD card in my dash cam?" "I had an accident, and there's no footage." These questions come up often. This guide covers what to check when choosing a dash cam card and how to maintain it. It doesn't recommend specific products, and we didn't test any cards. The models below are only examples of how to read a manual.

## What should you check first in a micro SD card for a dash cam?

Start with the memory card section of your dash cam's manual. Check four things.

| What to check | Example | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Card type | microSDHC, microSDXC | Fits the slot and the capacity standard |
| Capacity range | 8 to 512 GB | Cards outside the range aren't guaranteed to work |
| Speed class | Class 10 or higher | Minimum speed to write video without gaps |
| File system | FAT32, exFAT | The format the dash cam reads and writes |

Two Garmin manuals show how much this varies.

| Manual | Card type | Capacity | Speed class | File system |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Garmin Dash Cam X310](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-D9E2A866-74A8-4380-BE21-F1522E0149BC/EN-US/GUID-7D4A7BEE-124E-4D06-857C-8D497AFE81F4.html) | microSDHC, microSDXC | 8 to 512 GB | Class 10 or higher | exFAT |
| [Garmin Dash Cam 46/56/66W](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/dashcam46-56-66w/EN-US/GUID-7D4A7BEE-124E-4D06-857C-8D497AFE81F4.html) | microSDHC, microSDXC | 8 GB or greater | Class 10 or higher | FAT32 |

Both come from the same maker, yet one asks for exFAT and the other for FAT32. Don't copy another dash cam's requirements to yours.

## Which speed class does a dash cam SD card need?

Choose the class your manual lists, or higher. Both Garmin manuals above ask for Class 10 or higher. If your manual lists U3 or V30, choose a card with that marking.

A speed class number means a minimum write speed. The "up to XX MB/s" on the package isn't a guaranteed minimum. To read the markings, see [how to read SD card speed classes](/blog/en/sd-card-speed-class).

## How much capacity should a dash cam card have?

Stay within the range in your manual. A larger card keeps more footage before it's overwritten. Recording time also depends on resolution, the number of cameras (front and rear), and bit rate. Check your dash cam's manual or the maker's information for recording time by capacity.

Capacity also sets the card standard. Per the [SD Association's capacity page](https://www.sdcard.org/consumers/sd-sdhc-sdxc-and-sduc-card-capacity-choices/), cards up to 32 GB are SDHC, and cards over 32 GB are SDXC. If your dash cam supports only SDHC, don't use an SDXC card. For the differences, see [SDHC vs SDXC vs SDUC](/blog/en/sdhc-vs-sdxc).

## Does a dash cam need a high endurance SD card?

A dash cam records and overwrites the whole time the car is running. High endurance cards are built for this kind of repeated writing. SanDisk and Kingston datasheets list dash cams as a use for their high endurance cards.

High endurance means the card is designed to last longer, not that it can't fail. For how to read endurance ratings and why makers measure them differently, see [high endurance SD card](/blog/en/high-endurance-sd-card).

Some makers ask for their own cards. The [BlackVue manual](https://manual.blackvue.com/docs/dr770x-series-ii/system-updates-and-sd-card-maintenance/formatting-the-microsd-card-2/) says to use only official BlackVue microSD cards and that the company isn't responsible for problems caused by other makers' cards. Since support terms can depend on this, check your manual.

## How do you choose a micro SD card for a dash cam, step by step?

1. Find the memory card section in your dash cam's manual.
2. Check the card type and capacity range. Most dash cams take microSD. For size differences, see [micro SD card vs SD card](/blog/en/microsd-vs-sd).
3. Check the required speed class and choose that class or higher.
4. Note the file system the manual lists (FAT32 or exFAT).
5. See whether the maker names specific cards or keeps a recommended list. The Garmin X310 manual points to a list of recommended memory cards.
6. Buy from a seller you trust. A card with faked capacity may not save footage. To check, see [how to spot a fake SD card](/blog/en/fake-sd-card).

## What happens when a dash cam SD card is full?

The dash cam deletes the oldest unsaved footage. The [Garmin manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/dashcam45-55/EN-US/GUID-39BB7445-FF9C-4CC2-89AF-07311CAF07C9.html) says that when the card is full, the device automatically deletes the oldest unsaved video to make room. Saving a recording prevents it from being overwritten or deleted.

The [Garmin 46/56/66W manual](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/dashcam46-56-66w/EN-GB/GUID-E75CB7BE-ACAB-4427-A608-A4D9F919A911.html) sorts the card into these folders.

| Folder | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| 100EVENT | Videos saved automatically when an incident is detected |
| 102SAVED | Videos you saved manually |
| 103PARKM | Videos saved while parked |
| 105UNSVD | Unsaved footage (oldest is overwritten when space runs out) |

Folder names differ by model. Save the clips you need before they're overwritten, or copy them off the card. To connect the card to a computer, see [SD card reader for PC](/blog/en/sd-card-to-pc).

## How often should you format a dash cam SD card?

Follow your manual. Makers differ.

| Manual | Recommended interval | Other notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Garmin 46/56/66W](https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/dashcam46-56-66w/EN-US/GUID-E3DEB3E9-067E-4BD7-BAE9-D2F89366C6CD.html) | At least once every 6 months | Helps extend card life. Format a new card if it isn't FAT32 |
| [BlackVue DR770X Series II](https://manual.blackvue.com/docs/dr770x-series-ii/system-updates-and-sd-card-maintenance/formatting-the-microsd-card-2/) | Once a month | Also format if video quality looks poor. Use FAT32 |

Formatting erases all footage on the card. The BlackVue manual says this includes locked event files. Copy anything you want to keep first.

Check whether your dash cam can format the card itself. On the Garmin 46/56/66W, select Settings > Camera > Format Card and keep the camera powered until it finishes. If you format on a computer, choose the file system your manual lists. For computer steps, see [how to reformat a micro SD card](/blog/en/format-microsd).

## When should you replace a dash cam SD card?

The card is a consumable. In a 2018 report, [Japan's National Consumer Affairs Center](https://www.kokusen.go.jp/news/data/n-20180802_2.html) advised formatting SD cards regularly and replacing them with new ones regularly because they wear out. In the same report's survey, about 60% of respondents didn't know formatting or replacement was needed.

For a replacement interval, check your dash cam and card makers' information. Consider replacing the card when:

1. The dash cam reports a card error.
2. Video still breaks up or won't play after formatting.
3. Your computer doesn't recognize the card. For checks, see [SD card corrupted or not recognized](/blog/en/sd-card-not-working).

## Can a dash cam show it's recording but save nothing?

Yes. In the Center's tests with a card that couldn't be read or written, three products didn't detect the problem. They showed they were recording, but no footage was saved. The Center notes this result is one example under specific conditions.

That's why the Center advises checking recorded footage regularly. After you park, play back one recent clip.

## How do you view dash cam footage on an iPhone?

iPhone has no card slot, so connect the microSD card through a card reader. Per [Apple's external storage guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/external-storage-devices-iph95baac91f/ios), iPhone reads exFAT and FAT32. For connection steps and format checks, see [micro SD for iPhone](/blog/en/iphone-microsd).

The dash cam will overwrite its card again. Footage you've moved to your iPhone stays even after it's gone from the card. For a card going back into the dash cam, format it the way the dash cam manual says.

## FAQ: micro SD card for dash cam

### Can I use a microSD card from my phone in a dash cam?

Yes, if it matches the type, capacity, and speed class in the dash cam manual. Move the files off first, then format the card in the dash cam. Formatting erases your phone files.

### What happens if I never format my dash cam card?

The Garmin manual says regular formatting helps extend the card's life. The Center notes that some dash cams don't need regular formatting. Follow your model's manual.

### Is a 512 GB card better?

If your dash cam supports it, it keeps footage longer. Outside the supported range, the dash cam may not recognize it. The Garmin X310 supports 8 to 512 GB; other models differ.

### Can I format a dash cam card as exFAT?

Only if your manual lists exFAT. The Garmin 46/56/66W and BlackVue DR770X Series II manuals call for FAT32.

### Does a high endurance card skip formatting?

No. Follow your dash cam manual's formatting interval whatever card you use. High endurance cards can fail too. See [high endurance SD card](/blog/en/high-endurance-sd-card).
