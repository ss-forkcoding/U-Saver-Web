## Key takeaways

To use an iPhone SD card reader, plug it into the charging port and open one of two apps. Photos and videos from a camera go through the Photos app. Documents and other files go through the Files app.

Apple's camera adapters support SDSC, SDHC, SDXC, miniSD, microSD, and MMC cards.

If the import screen doesn't appear, disconnect the reader and the card, wait 30 seconds, and reconnect. Files that were renamed or edited may not import into Photos.

"I plugged in the reader and nothing happened." "Where do I open the PDF on my card?" These are common questions from people learning how to use an iPhone SD card reader. Which app you open depends on what you're moving. Reader models and card formats are covered in [Can you plug an SD card into an iPhone?](/blog/en/iphone-sd-card-slot)

## Which cards does an iPhone SD card reader read?

[Apple's guide to camera adapters](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118280) lists these cards and transfer protocols as supported.

| Item | Supported in Apple's guide |
| --- | --- |
| Cards | SDSC, SDHC, SDXC, miniSD, microSD, MMC |
| Protocols | Picture Transfer Protocol (PTP), Mass Storage Device protocol |
| Readers | Lightning to SD Card Camera Reader, Apple USB-C to SD Card Reader, and others |

The card needs a single data partition formatted as exFAT, FAT32, FAT, APFS, or macOS Extended (HFS+), according to [Apple's guide to external storage](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/external-storage-devices-iph95baac91f/ios). NTFS isn't on the list. To change the format, see [how to format a USB drive to exFAT](/blog/en/format-usb-exfat).

We haven't tested third-party card readers. If you use one, first check that the card appears under Locations in the Files app.

## Photos app or Files app: which one do you use?

It depends on the type of file.

| What you want to do | App | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Save camera photos and videos to your iPhone | Photos | [Apple's import guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/import-and-export-photos-and-videos-iph480caa1f3/ios) |
| Save iPhone photos and videos to the card | Photos | Export in the same guide |
| Move PDFs, documents, or zip files | Files | [Apple's guide to organizing files](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/organize-files-and-folders-iphab82e0798/ios) |
| Rename or erase the card | Files | [Apple's guide to transferring files](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/transfer-files-iphone-a-storage-device-iphe9aff429a/ios) |

Your iPhone doesn't show a pop-up when you plug in the reader. Open the app yourself.

## How to import photos with an iPhone SD card reader

These steps follow [Apple's import guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/import-and-export-photos-and-videos-iph480caa1f3/ios).

1. Insert the reader into the Lightning or USB-C connector on your iPhone.
2. Insert the SD card into the reader. It fits only one way.
3. Open the Photos app and tap Import.
4. Select the photos and videos, then select your import destination.
5. Tap Import All, or import only the items you selected.
6. When it's done, choose whether to keep or delete the originals on the card.

According to [Apple's guide to camera adapters](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118280), in iOS 18 you tap Collections in Photos, scroll to Devices, and tap the card. To export iPhone photos to a card, see [Can you plug an SD card into an iPhone?](/blog/en/iphone-sd-card-slot) and [how to transfer photos from iPhone to USB](/blog/en/iphone-photos-to-usb).

## How to copy files from an SD card in the Files app

Files that don't show up in Photos are handled in the Files app. These steps follow [Apple's guide to organizing files](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/organize-files-and-folders-iphab82e0798/ios).

1. Open the Files app and tap Browse.
2. Tap the card's name below Locations. If you don't see Locations, tap Browse again.
3. Touch and hold a file, then choose Copy or Move.
4. To move several files, tap the More button, tap Select, pick the files, then tap Move at the bottom.

To move iPhone files to the card, start the same steps from a folder on your iPhone. To rename or erase the card, touch and hold its name under Locations. Erasing deletes everything on the card. The same Files app workflow is in the [iPhone flash drive guide](/blog/en/iphone-flash-drive-guide).

When you're done, make sure the copy progress is gone, then remove the reader from the charging port. Apple's guide describes only removing it from the port.

## What should you check when an import fails?

These steps come from [Apple's guide to camera adapters](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118280).

| Order | What to check | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | iOS version | If you don't see Devices, update to the latest iOS |
| 2 | Reader | Disconnect the reader, wait 30 seconds, then reconnect |
| 3 | Card | Remove the card, wait 30 seconds, then reinsert it |
| 4 | iPhone | Restart your iPhone |
| 5 | Another card | Test with a different card. If it works, the data on the original card may be the problem |

If another card works but the original doesn't, Apple suggests importing the data to another device or computer first, then formatting the card in your camera. Formatting erases every photo on the card.

## Why are some photos or videos missing?

The same Apple guide gives two reasons.

- Files in a format that isn't compatible with iOS can't be viewed on the iPhone. You can import them to a computer later.
- Apple camera adapters import only content captured by digital cameras. Files that were renamed, edited, or copied onto the card may not import.

For those files, open the card in the Files app and copy them there. A FAT32 card can't store a file larger than 4 GB. For long videos, see [how to transfer videos from iPhone to USB](/blog/en/iphone-videos-to-usb).

## FAQ: how to use an iPhone SD card reader

### I plugged in the reader and nothing happened.

That's normal. Your iPhone doesn't show a pop-up. Look for the card under Import in Photos or under Locations in Files.

### Does it read microSD cards?

microSD is on the supported list in Apple's guide to camera adapters. Check that your reader has a slot for microSD.

### How fast is the transfer?

Apple's guide says iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro Max, iPhone 16 Pro, and iPhone 16 Pro Max support USB 3 transfer speeds, and that all other adapters and devices use USB 2 speeds. We haven't measured actual speeds.

### Where do I open PDFs or documents on the card?

In the Files app. Import in the Photos app is for photos and videos taken with a camera.

### Is the content of an SD card encrypted?

[Microsoft's comparison table](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/fileio/filesystem-functionality-comparison) lists no file system level encryption for exFAT. If you lose the card, someone else can read it, so store it carefully.
