## Key takeaways

A high endurance SD card is a microSD card made for devices that keep writing and overwriting video, like dash cams and home security cameras. Manufacturers state its endurance as "hours of Full HD recording" or as "P/E cycles."

Each manufacturer calculates recording hours differently. Samsung bases its figures on 26 Mbps video, and Kingston footnotes its 24/7 recording claims with 13 Mbps video. Don't compare the numbers directly.

High endurance doesn't mean fast. If you need 4K recording or quick copying, check the speed class (U3, V30) separately.

"My dash cam card died after a few months." "Is a high endurance SD card safer for storing photos?" These questions come up when people look for a high endurance SD card. This guide explains how to read the endurance figures on manufacturer data sheets and what to check, in order, when you choose a card. This guide doesn't recommend any product. The products below are used only as examples of how to read manufacturer documents.

## How is a high endurance SD card different from a regular card?

It's built to handle writing and rewriting for a long time. The [SanDisk High Endurance microSD data sheet](https://documents.sandisk.com/content/dam/asset-library/en_us/assets/public/sandisk/product/memory-cards/high-endurance-uhs-i-microsd/data-sheet-high-endurance-uhs-i-microsd.pdf) says writing and rewriting can take a toll on a memory card, and describes the card as made for recording and re-recording in dash cams and home monitoring security systems.

The [Kingston High-Endurance microSD data sheet](https://www.kingston.com/datasheets/SDCE_us.pdf) also targets write-intensive uses such as surveillance cameras, dash cams, and body cams.

| | Regular SD card | High endurance SD card |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main use the manufacturer states | Cameras, phones, file storage | Devices that record nonstop, like dash cams and security cameras |
| Endurance rating | Often not on the data sheet | Stated as recording hours or P/E cycles |
| Card size | SD, microSD | All three products checked here are microSD |

The [SD Association FAQ](https://www.sdcard.org/consumers/faq/) and [speed class page](https://www.sdcard.org/developers/sd-standard-overview/speed-class/) checked for this guide don't define an endurance class. "High endurance" is a product name that manufacturers use, and each manufacturer sets its own endurance criteria.

## How do you read a high endurance SD card's endurance rating?

Manufacturers use two kinds of figures.

1. **Recording hours:** How many hours of video recording the card is designed to handle. SanDisk and Samsung use this.
2. **P/E cycles:** How many times the flash memory can be programmed (written) and erased. Kingston says its card is rated to endure 3K P/E cycles.

Here's what the three data sheets say for 128GB.

| Manufacturer document | 128GB endurance rating | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [SanDisk High Endurance](https://documents.sandisk.com/content/dam/asset-library/en_us/assets/public/sandisk/product/memory-cards/high-endurance-uhs-i-microsd/data-sheet-high-endurance-uhs-i-microsd.pdf) | Up to 10,000 hours | Full HD (1920x1080) video; fewer hours for 4K |
| [Samsung PRO Endurance](https://download.semiconductor.samsung.com/resources/data-sheet/2024_Samsung_Data_sheet_microSD_Card_PRO_Endurance_v.1.1.pdf) | 70,080 hours | Full HD video at 26 Mbps (3.25MB/s) |
| [Kingston High-Endurance](https://www.kingston.com/datasheets/SDCE_us.pdf) | 3K P/E cycles, 26.9K operating hours | 24/7 recording over the 3-year warranty (the 24/7 claims are footnoted as Full HD at 13 Mbps) |

The SanDisk data sheet lists longer recording times for larger capacities: up to 2,500 hours for 32GB and up to 40,000 hours for 512GB. The Samsung data sheet also varies by capacity, from 17,520 hours for 32GB to 140,160 hours for 256GB.

## Can you compare recording hours across brands?

Not directly. The reference video bitrates differ. Samsung uses 26 Mbps video, and Kingston's footnote uses 13 Mbps. At a higher bitrate, the same card writes more data in the same amount of time.

The SanDisk data sheet says actual hours of video saved are less than stated, and fewer for 4K. The Samsung data sheet says video support may vary based on the host device, file attributes, and usage conditions.

| What to check | Where on the data sheet |
| --- | --- |
| Reference resolution | Full HD or 4K |
| Reference bitrate | Whether a Mbps value is given |
| Capacity | The same product has different hours by capacity |
| Warranty | SanDisk 2 years, Kingston 3 years, Samsung 2 to 5 years by capacity |

Find your own device's recording bitrate in the dash cam or camera manual.

## Is a high endurance SD card also fast?

Treat endurance and speed separately. High endurance products don't all have the same speed class.

| Manufacturer document | Speed class | Write speed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SanDisk High Endurance | Class 10, U3, V30 | Up to 40MB/s |
| Samsung PRO Endurance 32GB/64GB | Class 10, U1, V10 | Up to 30MB/s |
| Samsung PRO Endurance 128GB/256GB | Class 10, U3, V30 | Up to 40MB/s |
| Kingston High-Endurance | Class 10, U1, A1 | Up to 30 to 45MB/s by capacity |

The SD Association says speed class symbols with a number indicate minimum writing speed. If your device manual calls for V30, pick a card rated V30 or higher. How to read these markings is in [Class 10 SD Card, U3, and V30](/blog/en/sd-card-speed-class), and the A1 marking is covered in [A2 SD Card vs A1](/blog/en/sd-card-a1-a2).

## How do you choose a high endurance SD card?

1. **Read the device manual.** Check the card size it takes (microSD or not), the maximum capacity, and the speed class it needs.
2. **Match the capacity standard.** The Kingston data sheet lists the card format as FAT32 for SDHC and exFAT for SDXC. Check that your device supports SDXC. The difference is explained in [types of SD cards](/blog/en/what-is-sd-card).
3. **Match the speed class.** Pick the class your device requires or higher.
4. **Read the basis of the endurance figure.** Look at resolution, bitrate, and capacity together.
5. **Check the seller.** High endurance labels can be faked too. How to check capacity after you buy is in [Fake SD Card](/blog/en/fake-sd-card).

The Kingston data sheet says its flash cards are designed and tested for compatibility with consumer-grade products, and asks you to contact Kingston directly for special-use applications.

## Can you use a high endurance SD card with an iPhone?

Yes, through a reader. iPhone has no card slot, so you need an SD card reader. Use a card formatted in one of the formats listed in [Apple's guide to external storage](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/external-storage-devices-iph95baac91f/ios), such as exFAT or FAT32.

## High endurance SD card FAQ

### Can a high endurance SD card still fail?

Yes. The recording hours manufacturers give are "up to" figures or based on specific conditions. The SanDisk data sheet says actual hours are less than stated.

### Can I use a regular card in a dash cam instead?

Follow the card requirements in the device manual first. Manufacturers list dash cams and security cameras, which record nonstop, as the intended use for high endurance cards.

### Do more P/E cycles mean more recording hours?

That depends on bitrate and capacity. The Kingston data sheet lists 3K P/E cycles and 26.9K operating hours as separate figures. Read both.

### Are there high endurance cards in full SD size?

The SanDisk, Samsung, and Kingston products checked for this guide are all microSD. The SanDisk data sheet says the card includes an adapter for full-size SD devices.

### How do I check the remaining life of a high endurance SD card?

See whether the manufacturer offers a tool. The Kingston data sheet mentions an optional health monitoring tool to manage the card's lifespan.
