## Key takeaways

An SD card is a memory card you insert into cameras, phones, game consoles, and laptops. The SD Association sets the standards, and cards differ in three ways: size, capacity, and speed.

Before you buy an SD card, check which standards your device supports. A device that supports a newer standard reads cards of older standards, but not the other way around.

Format a new card in the device you'll use it in, and copy important photos to another storage device as well.

"What do SDXC, U3, V30, and A2 all mean?" "How do I plug a microSD card into my computer?" Questions pile up the first time you work with an SD card. This guide puts the SD card basics on one page and links to a detailed guide for each topic.

## What is an SD card?

An SD card is flash memory that keeps its data when the power is off. The [SD Association](https://www.sdcard.org/developers/sd-standard-overview/sd-family/) classifies SD memory cards as removable nonvolatile memory.

The camera, phone, or card reader you insert the card into is called the host. The card and the host must support the same standard to reach the card's full capacity and speed. Each marking on a card is explained in [types of SD cards](/blog/en/what-is-sd-card).

## What sizes do SD cards come in?

Two sizes are common today: full-size SD and microSD. The dimensions come from the [SD Association's capacity standards page](https://www.sdcard.org/developers/sd-standard-overview/capacity-sd-sdhc-sdxc-sduc/).

| Size | Width x height x thickness | Typical devices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SD | 32 x 24 x 2.1 mm | Digital cameras, laptop card slots |
| microSD | 11 x 15 x 1.0 mm | Phones, action cameras, handheld consoles |

The [SD Association FAQ](https://www.sdcard.org/consumers/faq/) says microSD and SD differ in size, number of pins, and whether there's a write-protect switch. The logic and electronics are identical.

## How do you read SD card capacity standards?

There are four capacity standards. You can tell them apart by the SD, SDHC, SDXC, or SDUC logo on the front of the card.

| Standard | Capacity | Default file system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SD | Up to 2 GB | FAT12, FAT16 |
| SDHC | Over 2 GB up to 32 GB | FAT32 |
| SDXC | Over 32 GB up to 2 TB | exFAT |
| SDUC | Over 2 TB up to 128 TB | exFAT |

According to the [SD Association's compatibility guide](https://www.sdcard.org/consumers/sd-sdhc-sdxc-and-sduc-card-capacity-choices/), compatibility only goes one way.

1. SDXC devices use SD, SDHC, and SDXC cards.
2. SDHC devices use only SD and SDHC cards.
3. SDUC cards work only in SDUC devices.

FAT32 can't store a file larger than 4 GB. The differences between standards and how to check your device are in [SDHC card vs SDXC vs SDUC](/blog/en/sdhc-vs-sdxc).

## What do the speed markings on an SD card mean?

An SD card can carry three different kinds of speed markings.

| Marking | Meaning | Examples | Detailed guide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bus interface | Maximum speed of the link between card and device | UHS-I, UHS-II, SD Express | [UHS SD card guide](/blog/en/uhs-sd-card) |
| Speed class | Minimum write speed for video recording | C10, U3, V30 | [how to read SD card speed classes](/blog/en/sd-card-speed-class) |
| App performance class | Performance for apps installed on the card | A1, A2 | [A2 SD card vs A1](/blog/en/sd-card-a1-a2) |

The [SD Association's speed class standard](https://www.sdcard.org/developers/sd-standard-overview/speed-class/) says the number indicates the minimum write speed. Choose a card with the same or a higher class than your device requires.

According to the [SD Association's bus speed page](https://www.sdcard.org/developers/sd-standard-overview/bus-speed-default-speed-high-speed-uhs-sd-express/), UHS-I reaches up to 104 MB/s, UHS-II up to 312 MB/s, and SD Express up to 3,940 MB/s. You only get that speed when the device and the card both support it. SD Express in the microSD size is covered in [what is microSD Express?](/blog/en/microsd-express)

The [application performance class page](https://www.sdcard.org/developers/sd-standard-overview/application-performance-class/) says A2 performance is available only when an A2 host and an A2 card are used together.

## What should you check before buying an SD card?

1. Check your device's manual for the card size (SD or microSD).
2. Check which capacity standard the device supports (SDHC, SDXC, SDUC).
3. Check the speed class the manual asks for.
4. If you'll move files to a computer, check the card reader's standards and connector.
5. After buying, check that the labeled capacity is actually usable.

Steps to check for a card with faked capacity are in [fake SD card](/blog/en/fake-sd-card). For devices that record nonstop, such as dash cams, also read [high endurance SD card](/blog/en/high-endurance-sd-card). Criteria for choosing a reader are in [how to choose an SD card reader](/blog/en/choose-sd-card-reader).

## How do you start using a new SD card?

1. Insert the card into the device you'll use it in.
2. Format the card from the device's menu. Formatting erases everything on the card.
3. Take a few photos or copy a file to confirm the card reads correctly.
4. Copy important photos to another storage device as well.

If you format on a computer, the SD Association [strongly recommends](https://www.sdcard.org/consumers/sd-sdhc-sdxc-and-sduc-card-capacity-choices/) the SD Memory Card Formatter over the generic formatting utilities that come with operating systems. Steps are in [SD Memory Card Formatter](/blog/en/sd-card-formatter).

## How do you connect an SD card to a PC, Mac, or iPhone?

Each device connects differently and has its own things to check.

| Device | How to connect | What to check | Detailed guide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Windows PC | Card slot or USB card reader | Use Safely Remove Hardware before removing | [SD card reader for PC](/blog/en/sd-card-to-pc) |
| Mac | Model with an SD slot, or a card reader | Eject before removing | [SD card reader for Mac](/blog/en/sd-card-on-mac) |
| USB-C devices | USB-C card reader | Card orientation, standards the reader supports | [how to use a USB C SD card reader](/blog/en/usb-c-sd-card-reader) |
| iPhone | SD card reader that fits the port | File format, number of partitions | [SD card reader for iPhone](/blog/en/iphone-sd-card-guide) |

[Microsoft's guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/safely-remove-hardware-in-windows-1ee6677d-4e6c-4359-efca-fd44b9cec369) says to use Safely Remove Hardware before disconnecting a device to avoid losing data. [Apple's Mac SD card slot guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102352) also says to eject the card before removing it and not to remove it while the Mac is asleep.

An iPhone has no SD card slot. [Apple's camera adapter guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118280) lists SDSC, SDHC, SDXC, miniSD, microSD, and MMC cards as supported. [Apple's external storage guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/external-storage-devices-iph95baac91f/ios) says the device must have a single data partition and use a format such as APFS, exFAT, or FAT32.

Using a USB flash drive on a computer works much the same way. See [how to use a flash drive on a computer](/blog/en/usb-flash-drive-basics).

## SD card FAQ

### What's the difference between an SD card and a microSD card?

According to the SD Association FAQ, they differ in size, number of pins, and whether there's a write-protect switch. The logic and electronics are identical.

### Why won't a 64 GB SD card work in my old camera?

A 64 GB card is SDXC. Devices that support only SDHC can't use SDXC cards. Check your camera's manual for SDXC support.

### What should I use to format an SD card?

Format it in the device you'll use it in first. If you format on a computer, the SD Association recommends the SD Memory Card Formatter.

### Can I just pull an SD card out of my computer?

Use Safely Remove Hardware on Windows or eject the card on a Mac first. Removing a card while it's in use can cause data loss.

### Can I use an SD card with an iPhone?

Yes, through an SD card reader that fits your iPhone's port. The card needs a single data partition and a supported format such as exFAT or FAT32. Details are in [SD card reader for iPhone](/blog/en/iphone-sd-card-guide).
