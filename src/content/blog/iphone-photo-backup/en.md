## Key takeaways

The best way to save photos from iPhone without iCloud depends on the equipment you have. You can export originals from the Photos app to an external drive such as a USB drive, or import them to a Mac or Windows PC with a USB cable.

If you don't have a computer, a USB drive is the simplest option. Export Unmodified Originals in the Photos app saves the files as they were captured.

On a Mac, you import with the Photos app. On a Windows PC, you install the Apple Devices app from the Microsoft Store first, then import with the Microsoft Photos app.

If iCloud Photos is turned on, make sure the originals are downloaded to your iPhone first. If they aren't on the device, your PC may not be able to import them.

"My iCloud storage is full and I want to keep my photos somewhere else." "Is there a way to protect my photos without paying every month?" "Do I need a computer?" These are common questions from people looking for the best way to save photos from iPhone. This guide compares the methods, walks through each one, and covers what to check afterward.

## What's the best way to save photos from iPhone without iCloud?

The methods differ in where your photos go. None of them uses online storage.

| Criteria | Export to a USB drive | Import to a Mac | Import to a Windows PC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Computer | Not needed | Mac required | Windows PC required |
| Apps | Photos and Files on iPhone | Photos on Mac | Apple Devices, Microsoft Photos |
| Connection | USB drive, plus an adapter if needed | USB cable | USB cable |
| Where photos go | The USB drive | The Photos library on the Mac | A PC folder you choose |
| Source | [Apple: download photos](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111762) | [Apple: transfer photos](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120267) | [Microsoft: import photos](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/import-photos-and-videos-from-phone-to-pc-198f2301-e9a7-c734-5f39-a8946a5ebc99) |

According to [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120267), photos and videos in iCloud Photos use your iCloud storage. [Apple's iCloud+ plans page](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108047) says you get 5 GB for free, and more storage requires a monthly iCloud+ plan. Keeping photos on your own storage gives you one more copy that doesn't depend on that space.

## What should you check before you save photos?

Checking these three things first helps keep the transfer from stalling partway.

| What to check | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Whether iCloud Photos is on | If it is, download the originals to your iPhone before importing to a PC |
| The drive's format | iPhone reads drives with a single data partition in specific formats such as exFAT and FAT32 |
| Videos over 4 GB | FAT32 can't store files larger than 4 GB |

[Apple says](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120267) that if iCloud Photos is on, you must download the original, full-resolution photos to your iPhone before you import to your PC. [Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/import-photos-and-videos-from-phone-to-pc-198f2301-e9a7-c734-5f39-a8946a5ebc99) also notes that photos stored in iCloud but not on the device may not download.

Supported formats are listed in [Apple's guide to external storage](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/external-storage-devices-iph95baac91f/ios). The FAT32 file size limit comes from [Microsoft's file system comparison](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/fileio/filesystem-functionality-comparison). If the drive will hold videos too, format it as exFAT using [how to format a USB drive to exFAT](/blog/en/format-usb-exfat).

## How to save photos from iPhone to a USB drive without a computer

These steps follow [Apple's guide to downloading photos](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111762).

1. Connect the USB drive to your iPhone.
2. In the Photos app, go to your Library.
3. Tap Select, then choose the photos and videos you want to save.
4. Tap the Share button, then tap Export Unmodified Originals.
5. The Files app opens to iCloud Drive. Tap the Back arrow and choose the drive under Locations.
6. Tap Save.

Apple recommends updating to the latest iOS and connecting to Wi-Fi first. If the originals are only in iCloud, they need time to download. If you have a lot of photos, save them in batches by month or year. If a transfer stops, it's easier to see where it left off.

If the drive doesn't appear in Files, check the connection steps in [how to use a flash drive with an iPhone](/blog/en/iphone-flash-drive-guide). For a screen-by-screen walkthrough, see [how to transfer photos from iPhone to a USB drive](/blog/en/iphone-photos-to-usb).

## How to save photos from iPhone to a Mac

These steps follow [Apple's guide to transferring photos](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120267).

1. Connect your iPhone to your Mac with a USB cable. If you're asked to allow the accessory to connect, click Allow.
2. Open the Photos app on your Mac. If the Import screen doesn't appear, click your iPhone's name in the sidebar.
3. If asked, unlock your iPhone with your passcode. If Trust This Computer appears, tap Trust.
4. Next to Album, choose an existing album or create a new one.
5. Select photos and click Import Selected, or click Import All New Items.
6. Wait for the import to finish, then disconnect your iPhone.

The Photos library on your Mac is still a single copy on one machine. Copy the imported photos to an external drive as well, so a copy survives if the Mac fails.

## How to save photos from iPhone to a Windows PC

These steps follow [Apple's guide to transferring photos](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120267) and [Microsoft's guide to importing photos](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/import-photos-and-videos-from-phone-to-pc-198f2301-e9a7-c734-5f39-a8946a5ebc99).

1. Install the Apple Devices app from the Microsoft Store.
2. Connect your iPhone to the PC with a USB cable that can transfer files.
3. Turn on your iPhone and unlock it. Your PC can't find the device if it's locked.
4. If Trust This Computer appears on your iPhone, tap Trust or Allow.
5. On your PC, select the Start button, then select Photos.
6. Select Import, choose your connected iPhone, and follow the instructions. You can pick the items to import and where to save them.

If your PC often can't open the photos, you can change how your iPhone captures them. According to Apple, go to Settings, tap Camera, tap Formats, then tap Most Compatible. From then on, your camera captures photos and videos in JPEG and H.264. Photos you've already taken keep their format.

## Which method should you choose?

Choose based on the equipment you have and how many photos you keep.

| Situation | Method |
| --- | --- |
| No computer | Export to a USB drive |
| You use a Mac | Import with Photos on the Mac, then copy to an external drive |
| You use a Windows PC | Import with Microsoft Photos, then copy to another drive |
| Lots of videos over 4 GB | Use a drive formatted as exFAT |

Whatever you choose, a copy kept in only one place is lost if that device fails. Keep important photos in at least two places. ## Four things to check before you delete photos from your iPhone

If any of these is a "no," don't delete photos from your iPhone yet.

- The photos actually open from where you saved them.
- A long video plays all the way to the end.
- The number of saved files is the same as or more than the number of photos you selected.
- Important photos also have a copy on a second drive.

According to [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111762), when iCloud Photos is on, deleting a photo from your device also removes it from iCloud and from your other devices with iCloud Photos turned on. Check this first if you're deleting photos to free up space.

## FAQ: best way to save photos from iPhone

### Can I save photos from iPhone without iCloud?

Yes. Export originals from the Photos app to a USB drive, or import them to a Mac or Windows PC with a USB cable. None of these methods uses iCloud storage.

### Does saving iPhone photos to a USB drive lower the quality?

Export Unmodified Originals saves the files as they were captured. Photos you edited in the Photos app are saved without the edits, so check separately if you need the edited versions.

### My Windows PC can't find my iPhone.

Make sure your iPhone is unlocked. Microsoft says your PC can't find the device if it's locked. Not every USB cable can transfer files, so try a different cable too.

### Some photos are missing after importing to my PC.

If iCloud Photos is on, photos stored in iCloud but not on your iPhone may not import. As Apple advises, download the full-resolution originals to your iPhone first, then import again.

### Can I download my iCloud Photos to a Mac as originals?

Yes. According to [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111762), open Photos on your Mac, choose Photos, then Settings, select iCloud, and click Download Originals to this Mac. Your whole library downloads in full resolution.
